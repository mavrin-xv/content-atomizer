# How our clients close the month in 3 days instead of 9

*Northwind Books is an invented bookkeeping firm for small retail shops. This article is sample input for the pack.*

Most of our clients are shops with one to five staff: a bike repair shop, two bakeries, a plant store. When they joined us, the average one closed its books 9 working days after the month ended. The owner saw last month's numbers in the middle of this month, which is too late to change anything.

Today the same 14 clients close in 3 working days. Nobody hired anyone. We changed four habits, and none of them is software.

## 1. Reconcile every day, for 10 minutes

The month-end pile is made of small things nobody looked at: a card refund, a supplier who charged twice, a cash deposit with no note. At month end each one takes 20 minutes of detective work, because nobody remembers.

Our clients now open the bank feed every morning and match yesterday's lines. It takes about 10 minutes. Anything that does not match gets a one-line note while the memory is fresh. At month end there is almost nothing left to explain.

## 2. One inbox for receipts

Receipts used to live in four places: a shoebox, the owner's phone, a supplier portal and email. We give each client one email address that only receives receipts. Staff forward or photograph everything to it on the day they spend the money.

The rule is simple: if it is not in the receipts inbox by Friday, it does not exist. The first month is painful. By the second month, missing receipts drop from about 30 a month to under 5.

## 3. Close on a calendar, not when it is done

"We close when everything is in" means you never close. Each client now has three fixed dates in the calendar:

- Day 1: bank and card reconciliation finished
- Day 2: payroll and supplier bills checked
- Day 3: the owner gets a one-page report and a 15-minute call

If something is still missing on day 3, we close anyway with a note and fix it next month. A late correction of a small amount is cheaper than a late report.

## 4. A one-page report the owner reads

The old report was a 12-page export nobody opened. The new one fits on one page and answers three questions: how much cash is there, what changed against last month, and what needs a decision this week.

The 15-minute call matters more than the page. The owner says what they will do about the number, and we write it down. Next month we start the call with that line.

## What it cost

Setting this up took each client about 2 hours with us in the first week, plus the 10 minutes a day. The hard part was not the setup. It was the first month of saying no to "I'll find that receipt later".

## What it did not fix

A fast close does not make a shop profitable. Two of the 14 clients still have a cash problem; they just see it 6 days earlier. That is the point. You cannot fix a number you see too late.

If you run a small shop, start with the 10-minute daily reconciliation. It is the habit that makes the other three possible.
