## x: thread
14 small shops used to close their books 9 days after month end.

Now they close in 3. Nobody hired anyone, and nobody bought software.

Here are the four habits that did it:

---
1/ Reconcile every morning, for 10 minutes.

The month-end pile is a stack of tiny mysteries: a refund, a double charge, a deposit with no note. Each one costs 20 minutes at month end because nobody remembers. On the day, it costs a one-line note.

---
2/ One inbox for receipts, and only receipts.

Shoebox, phone, supplier portal, email: pick none of them. Everything goes to one address on the day the money is spent.

Rule: not in the inbox by Friday, it does not exist.

---
3/ Close on a calendar, not when it is done.

Day 1 bank and cards. Day 2 payroll and bills. Day 3 the owner gets the report.

Still missing something on day 3? Close anyway with a note. A small late correction is cheaper than a late report.

---
4/ Replace the 12-page export with one page.

Three questions: how much cash, what changed, what needs a decision this week. Then a 15-minute call where the owner says what they will do.

---
What it did not fix: 2 of the 14 still have a cash problem.

They see it 6 days earlier. You cannot fix a number you see too late.

If you change one thing, start with the 10 minutes in the morning.

## telegram: long-post
One of our bakery clients used to see last month's numbers in the middle of this month. By then half the month was gone, and so was the chance to change anything.

That was normal. Across our 14 small-shop clients, the books closed 9 working days after month end. Today the same shops close in 3. No new hires, no new software. Four habits:

**Every morning, 10 minutes with the bank feed.** Match yesterday's lines, write one note for anything odd. Month end stops being detective work.

**One receipts inbox.** Not the shoebox, not the phone. One address, forwarded on the day of the spend. Missing receipts went from about 30 a month to under 5 by the second month. The first month is the painful one.

**Three fixed dates.** Day 1 reconciliation, day 2 payroll and bills, day 3 a one-page report and a 15-minute call. If something is missing, we close anyway and fix it next month.

**A page the owner actually reads.** Cash, change against last month, decisions for this week. On the call the owner says what they will do, and next month's call starts with that line.

Setup took each shop about 2 hours with us in the first week.

The honest part: 2 of the 14 still have a cash problem. The fast close did not fix it. They just see it 6 days earlier, which is the only way it gets fixed.

If you run a shop, try the 10-minute morning match for two weeks and tell me what you found in the pile.
